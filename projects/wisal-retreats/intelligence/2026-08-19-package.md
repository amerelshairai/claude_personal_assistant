## 1. Executive Summary

Mohammed Mikdad ("Moe Meqdad"), selling premium wellness retreats that combine

## 14. Recommended Features

The main recommendation — each item traces to a gold bar above:

1. **Unified AI first-responder** across WhatsApp Business, Instagram DM, and
   Messenger that answers routine questions (pricing, dates, availability,
   general retreat info) automatically. *Effort + Time.*
2. **High-value lead scoring** against criteria Mohammed defines (see §26 Q2) —
   flagged leads are surfaced for Mohammed's personal attention while the AI
   keeps handling routine ones. *Effort + Profit* — protects his limited
   personal-touch capacity for the leads that matter most.
3. **Central lead database** storing contact info, platform of origin, full
   conversation history, and tracked preferences ("desire" — which retreat,
   which questions, stated hesitations). *Effort + Profit.*
4. **Scoped proactive outreach to flagged high-value leads only** — directly
   answers the "contact them" requirement for the one case that's unambiguous
   (a flagged high-value lead who's gone quiet gets a follow-up), without
   assuming it means blanket automated outreach to everyone. See §15 for the
   broader version.
5. **Basic FAQ automation** for repetitive questions. *Time/Effort.*

**Phased rollout recommendation** (ties to §21 adoption risk): start with an
Instagram-only pilot — it's the clearly more active, primary channel (4,885
followers, regular posting) — covering items 1–3 above, before expanding to
WhatsApp and Messenger. Given Mohammed's stated low trust in outside help, a
working, visible pilot on one channel is likely to build the trust needed for
him to hand over the rest, rather than asking him to commit to all three
platforms at once.

  requirement on its own, but is exactly the phased approach recommended in
  §14/§23 as a trust-building first step rather than a rejected alternative.
- **Hire a human virtual assistant/social-media manager instead of building

  detection" requirement, and doesn't resolve Mohammed's stated trust concern —
  arguably an AI system he can inspect and adjust is a better fit for a
  low-trust posture than handing conversations to a new person — **INFERRED,
  Medium confidence**.

## 23. Recommended Solution

A **phased, multi-platform AI lead-response and high-value-lead-detection
system with a central lead database**, starting with an Instagram-only pilot
(the primary, most active channel) covering auto-response, lead scoring, and
the database, then expanding to WhatsApp and Messenger once the pilot has
demonstrated value and built trust. This wins over a single big-bang
multi-platform build because it directly manages the one adoption risk that's
explicitly known about this client (§21) while still delivering on every piece
of the stated requirement over time.

**`automation-architecture`** needs to design the phased architecture: Phase 1
— Instagram (Meta Graph API) ingestion, AI auto-response, lead scoring, database
write. Phase 2 — add WhatsApp Business API and Messenger. Flag that WhatsApp
Business API / Meta app review and business verification can carry real
calendar lead-time independent of build hours — this affects `project-planning`
scheduling, not just effort estimation. Non-negotiable design elements (error
handling, retry, logging, human-approval points) apply as usual, with particular
attention to the Meta policy-violation risk (§21) — this needs its own
mitigation design, not just a generic risk note.

**`business-analysis`** needs to price this without a dollar ROI to lean on —
the pitch is effort/time-value-led (§16/§17), which changes how the value case
is framed in a proposal versus a client with hard cost data. Phased delivery
(pilot vs. full build) likely implies a phased pricing structure too — worth
deciding explicitly rather than pricing the whole multi-platform build as one
lump sum.

**`project-planning`** needs two phases as separate milestones (Instagram pilot;
WhatsApp+Messenger expansion), with the API verification lead-time flagged as a
schedule risk, and the 8 items in §25/§26 flagged as blocking Phase 1 kickoff —
especially the high-value-lead definition (§26 Q2), without which the scoring
logic can't be built at all.
